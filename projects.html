Inspect everything, or nothing?

1,567 production runs from a real semiconductor line, each with 590 sensor readings. Only 104 runs failed (6.6%), and the failure rate changed a lot over the three months the data covers.

The break-even is clear

Inspecting everything costs the same at any cost ratio. Inspecting nothing gets more expensive the more an escaped failure costs. The two lines cross at a cost ratio of about 18.

The models could not get below it

The models barely separated failing runs from passing ones. Three of the four were never cheaper than both simple policies. The random forest here is typical.

Why: the line kept changing

Sensor s059 separated failures well within the training period, but that link disappeared in the test period. None of the ways I tried of handling the drift helped.

Sold out for eleven years

Airbus has more than 7,000 A320neo Family aircraft on order, but delivered only 607 in 2025. So it plans to raise production from about 51 aircraft a month to 75.

Three points decide it

The ramp-up is worth €1.0bn if suppliers support 85% of the plan, and breaks even at 82%. The base case sits only three points above break-even.

Supplier availability matters most

I moved every assumption to its downside and upside value in turn. Supplier availability has the widest range of any input.

The money goes out years before it comes back

The investment is made before the extra aircraft are delivered, so the low point is −€2.7bn in 2027.

  1. Make High-NA pay for customers. ASML's advantage rests on technology leadership. The priority is throughput, availability and total cost of ownership, because customers adopt a new machine when the economics work.
  2. Use the installed base as the steady earner. Service, upgrades and field options bring lower-risk recurring revenue. They should support investment in new technology, not replace it.
  3. Integrate only where supply could hold High-NA back. Broad vertical integration would be costly and inefficient. Target the few components where supplier concentration is a real risk.

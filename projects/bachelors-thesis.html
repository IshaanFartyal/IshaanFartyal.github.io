Determination of a Transition Plane in Gas Atomization

Investigating how droplet properties evolve downstream in a gas atomization simulation to determine a transition plane that balances physical reliability with computational efficiency.

OpenFOAM CFD VOF Data Analysis Thermo-Fluids
View Thesis ↗
Bachelor Final Project / Power & Flow
20 mm recommended transition plane
01 Situation

Coupling two atomization models

In gas atomization, the primary breakup phase can be resolved using a Volume-of-Fluid model, while the secondary breakup phase can be treated using Lagrangian particle tracking. A transition plane is therefore required to transfer droplet data between the two numerical models.

02 Task

Find the earliest reliable transition location

The objective was to determine where droplets had become sufficiently developed for transfer to the secondary-breakup model, while keeping the primary-breakup simulation domain as short as possible to avoid unnecessary computational cost.

03 Action

Analyse droplet evolution across two CFD cases

I analysed OpenFOAM VOF-cloud data from two gas-atomization cases with different materials, gases, nozzle geometries and initial conditions.

  • Grouped droplet data into 1 mm axial-distance bins.
  • Used medians and interquartile ranges to reduce sensitivity to noisy data.
  • Evaluated droplet diameter, axial velocity and temperature versus downstream distance.
  • Investigated multiple sphericity criteria and Weber number.
  • Used breakup length as a lower-bound constraint for the transition location.
  • Compared trends across both simulation cases before forming a recommendation.
04 Result

Recommend a 20 mm transition plane

The analysis supported a transition plane 20 mm downstream from the nozzle for the studied cases. The broader selection rule was to use the earliest location where droplet diameter and temperature trend downward, axial velocity trends upward, and breakup length is satisfied as a lower bound.

Engineering implication

The recommendation provides a practical balance between simulation reliability and computational efficiency when coupling the primary and secondary atomization models.

05. Analytical Framework
Droplet Size Diameter

Assessed whether droplets became smaller and approached a stable downstream trend.

Momentum Axial velocity

Tracked acceleration of droplets as momentum transferred from the surrounding gas.

Thermal Temperature

Evaluated droplet cooling as the liquid travelled downstream through the atomizing gas.

Constraint Breakup length

Used the end of the continuous liquid core as a minimum feasible transition location.

06. Limitations & Follow-Up
Sphericity Inconclusive metrics

The available geometric measures did not establish a clear relationship between sphericity and axial distance.

Weber Number Approximate inputs

Gas density and surface tension were approximated rather than evaluated locally within the flow field.

Simulation Domain More downstream data

A longer axial domain would provide additional data points and strengthen trend validation.

Generalisability Additional cases

Further material, gas and geometry combinations would be needed to develop a broader transition-plane rule.

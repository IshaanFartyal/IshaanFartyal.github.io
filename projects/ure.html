01 Context

A race car is also a business case

Formula Student teams design and build an electric race car every year. They are judged on more than speed: in the Cost event, a team has to defend what its car costs and how it would be manufactured.

I spent 2024–25 as a Mechanical Design Engineer and 2025–26 as a Cost Engineer, so I saw the same car from both sides.

03 Cost · 2025–26

What does it cost to build one, or five thousand?

As Cost Engineer I maintained the car's Bill of Materials and its costed, carbonised version, which track the cost and lifecycle emissions of every part.

The Cost event also asks how a prototype would be mass-produced. I took the inverter as the test case and built a model in Excel and MATLAB comparing four ways to make it.

By hand

A small team assembles each inverter. Almost no equipment, but 16 hours of work per unit.

By machine

A €70,000 pick-and-place machine populates the circuit boards. High upfront cost, low cost per unit.

In-house

The team carries the fixed costs itself: equipment, floor space, training and development.

Outsourced

A supplier carries them instead. Hand assembly is priced at cost plus a margin; machine assembly at a fixed price per unit.

What the model shows

At low volume the fixed costs dominate, so cost per unit falls steeply as volume grows. In-house hand assembly then levels off at about €1,200 an inverter, because the labour does not get cheaper with scale. The in-house machine route keeps falling, with a small step up each time another machine is needed. Outsourced machine assembly is a flat €10,000 an inverter, an estimate from the department manager, so it only makes sense for the first few units.

04 Result

Taken to two competitions by the team

The work went onto the "Mass Production vs Prototyping" poster, which my teammates presented at Formula Student Czech Republic and at Formula Student Germany, the largest Formula Student competition in the world.

How far to trust it

The inverter model is a first-pass comparison built on estimates. The four routes do not yet carry identical cost items, so I present the shape of the result here, not a break-even volume.

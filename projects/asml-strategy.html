ASML Technology Strategy

Assessing how ASML should allocate strategic priorities to sustain its competitive advantage through the next generation of semiconductor lithography.

Advanced Lithography / Strategic Analysis
3 Strategic priorities evaluated
01 Situation

Strong market position, but new strategic pressures

ASML holds a uniquely strong position in advanced lithography, supported by high barriers to entry, accumulated technical expertise, a specialized supplier ecosystem and limited competition in leading-edge EUV. At the same time, the next stage of value creation depends on successful High-NA commercialization, geopolitical adaptability and supplier scalability.

02 Task

Prioritize the strategic actions that sustain long-term advantage

The objective was to determine which strategic initiatives should receive the greatest attention as ASML transitions from established EUV leadership toward High-NA EUV, while continuing to capture value from its installed base and manage structural supply-chain risk.

03 Action

Combine industry, financial and technology analysis

I assessed ASML's business model and competitive environment, then linked those findings to operating and financial performance before comparing alternative strategic courses of action.

  • Analysed ASML's revenue model across system sales and installed-base management.
  • Assessed competitive forces, barriers to entry and supplier/customer power.
  • Reviewed revenue growth, product mix, R&D intensity, margins and end-use exposure.
  • Evaluated High-NA commercialization as both a technology and customer-economics challenge.
  • Assessed geopolitical exposure and concentrated supplier dependency.
  • Compared three strategic options by strategic value, investment, risk and time horizon.
04 Result

Prioritize High-NA, then installed-base value capture and supplier resilience

The analysis recommends that ASML place its highest strategic priority on accelerating High-NA commercialization and improving customer economics, while using installed-base monetization as a lower-risk source of recurring revenue and strengthening supplier resilience through selective vertical integration.

Strategic recommendation

Focus first on throughput, availability, process repeatability and total cost of ownership for High-NA; second on recurring value from service, upgrades and field options; and third on targeted integration where supplier concentration creates disproportionate operational risk.

05. Performance Snapshot
Revenue €18.6bn → €32.7bn

Revenue growth from 2021 to 2025, reflecting continued expansion in advanced lithography.

Installed Base 25% of 2025 sales

Service and field-option revenue provides a growing recurring-revenue base.

R&D €4.7bn in 2025

R&D expenditure remained around 14–15% of revenue while absolute spend increased.

Margin 50–53%

Gross margin remained resilient across 2021–2025, supporting ASML's strong value capture.

06. Strategic Priorities
Priority 1 Accelerate High-NA commercialization

Improve throughput, availability and customer economics to accelerate adoption.

Priority 2 Expand installed-base monetization

Increase recurring value from service, upgrades, field options and productivity solutions.

Priority 3 Selective vertical integration

Reduce exposure where supplier concentration could constrain production growth.

Constraint Geopolitical adaptability

Manage export-control exposure through geographic diversification and regulatory flexibility.

07. Key Risks
Adoption Slower High-NA uptake

Upfront investment may be realized before commercial returns if qualification and adoption take longer than expected.

Supply Chain Supplier scaling constraints

Critical suppliers may become bottlenecks as High-NA production complexity increases.

Geopolitics Restricted market access

Export controls can alter sales timing, product mix and accessible customer markets.

Innovation Over-focus on installed base

Excessive emphasis on monetization could weaken long-term technological leadership if it displaces future R&D.

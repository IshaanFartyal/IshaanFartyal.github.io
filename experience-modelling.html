Analytical modelling

University projects where the main work was building a model and checking it against reality.

What happens inside the cylinder

We ran a small generator engine on petrol–ethanol blends from E0 to E15 at different loads, measured the pressure in the cylinder, and built models to predict it. Most of the data side was mine.

Fuels
E0, E5, E10, E15
Loads
None, half, full
Tools
MATLAB

My contribution

  • Built the cylinder volume model from the engine's measured dimensions.
  • Turned the raw sensor voltages into pressure, and found the crank position from the double-tooth signal.
  • Worked out the offsets between peak pressure, top dead centre and that signal, and dealt with pressure drift.
  • Cut the recordings into single engine cycles and averaged the trials, with their uncertainty.
  • Added the air–fuel ratio for each blend to our detailed model, and analysed the efficiency trends.
  • With a teammate, made the model-versus-experiment comparisons for the report.
1 / 5

How it turned out

The simple, ideal model was far off in both pressure and shape. Our detailed model, with heat losses and the right air–fuel ratio, matched the measurements closely.

Efficiency rose with load, as expected. Our measurements also showed it rising with ethanol content, but by far more than theory allows. The detailed model kept it almost flat, and I'd trust it over our data here: the fuel consumption measurements, especially for E0, are the likelier problem.

Storing sunshine as compressed air

We modelled a household with solar panels that stores its surplus as compressed air, to see how much less it would need to buy from the grid. I worked on the MATLAB/Simulink model; teammates built and ran the experiment to validate it.

The model

I analysed the household's energy data and set the losses in the compressor, storage, extraction and transport. So much energy is lost as heat when air is compressed that I added a heat exchanger block, which stores that heat and gives it back during expansion. It took a fair bit of debugging, and the gain was small but real.

What it showed

The share of energy the house bought from the grid fell from 50% to 32%. Only about 20% of what went into storage came back out, mostly lost in compression and expansion.
